# FSFFL NEXT - Pre-merge elite RB Year-3 conditional-production diagnostic

Date: 2026-09-19

## Final classification

**B. PLAUSIBLE BUT UNCERTAIN - MANAGEMENT JUDGMENT REQUIRED**

## Plain-language answer

P0 cuts high-end RB Year-3 conditional production mainly because the frozen production learner forecasts an absolute future point level rather than preserving a fixed percentage of an unusually high source season. Source magnitude enters through a sublinear log term and a bounded percentile, while pooled position/state baselines carry substantial weight. That creates regression toward historically typical future production levels.

Across the exact nine current top-decile RBs, about 71.0% of the total Year-1-to-Year-3-expected decline is already present in conditional production/state means, 9.4% comes from future-state composition, and 19.6% comes from disappearance/persistence. Persistence is therefore not the main explanation.

The historical evidence supports the direction and aggregate magnitude. In the frozen 2014-2020 selection window, active top-decile RBs realized a median Year-3 conditional retention of 0.549; the current nine-player board is 0.562. Across the broader 2005-2022 final-fit support, the active top-decile RB median is 0.583.

The uncertainty is concentrated in the extreme/veteran tail. Only two active veteran top-decile RB rows exist in the final-fit historical support, and their source production topped out at 180.2 points, while Christian McCaffrey and Derrick Henry enter the current frozen source coordinate at 276.7 and 252.05 standard points. Post-freeze 2021-2022 replication was also materially more optimistic than P0.

## Mechanism findings

- Conditional production/state means are the primary compression mechanism.
- State mixing is secondary and applies only to D1; D0 has one common positive-state mean.
- Prior-two D1 features are tiny for Gibbs, Bijan, and Chase Brown and are not the driver.
- Direct percentile contributions are positive in the relevant high-end predictions; compression is not caused by a hidden negative percentile penalty.
- Derrick Henry has a meaningful negative age/experience contribution; Christian McCaffrey's is much smaller.
- The full RB distribution shows a broad decline in retention as source production rises, rather than a discontinuity created at the top-decile threshold.
- Developmental RB D1 was historically supported in every frozen chronology block. Established RB D1 failed the chronology rule, so D0 remained selected. Veteran D0 is a sparse fallback.
- Current top-end RB compression is not mirrored at the same magnitude for QB, WR, or TE.

## Why classification B rather than A, C, or D

A is too strong because veteran high-end evidence is extremely sparse, current veteran source magnitude extrapolates beyond the observed active veteran support, and the post-freeze replication is more optimistic.

C is not supported because the aggregate historical top-decile RB behavior is close to the current board, the mechanism is coherent with the frozen architecture, and no new systematic mechanical failure was found.

D is not supported because the approved 335-player board reproduced, all frozen hashes/route identities match, and the serialized P0 scorer is being executed as intended.

## Merge implication

This diagnostic identifies no implementation defect or implementation blocker. It is not itself merge authorization. A separate merge review should explicitly accept the residual uncertainty in the high-end veteran RB tail.

No model, route, coefficient, feature, scoring, implementation-code, merge, or deploy change was made.
