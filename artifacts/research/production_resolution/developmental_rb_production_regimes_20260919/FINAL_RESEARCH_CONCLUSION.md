# Final research conclusion

Classification: D. INDIVIDUAL SUSTAINABILITY NOT RELIABLY IDENTIFIABLE - DISTRIBUTIONAL UNCERTAINTY IS NEXT

Exceptional developmental-RB source seasons do show a different high-end forecasting shape. In frozen 2014-2020 Y3 active outcomes, absolute future points have a stable upward slope change near the season-relative p90 source-magnitude coordinate. P90 was the leave-one-source-season-out best tested knot in 6 of 7 seasons, but bootstrap boundary mass remained spread across adjacent candidate knots, so a hard tier is not sufficiently stable.

The discrete D1T90 tier fails the frozen chronology/uncertainty gates. The continuous D1H90 hinge is more promising: it improves pooled Y3 CRPS with a positive 95% interval and improves MAE, while doing no harm below p90. However, it fails the preregistered high-magnitude bias-reduction gate, so it does not earn authority. Locked 2021-2022 shows the same direction: Y3 CRPS is slightly better than D1, but the exceptional high-source retention gap remains too large.

Within the exceptional-magnitude region, dpts does not show a reliable negative Y3 effect after conditioning on source level. The globally negative spike-regression interpretation therefore should not be treated as a proven transient-breakout signal in that regime.

The additional ex-ante signal audit also fails. Source opportunity, workload/efficiency, availability, and prior-role stability do not produce stable chronological discrimination between productive survivors and major declines beyond source magnitude plus existing D1 information. No richer sustainable-breakout classifier is authorized.

The research therefore does not support a hard elite tier or a promoted continuous mean correction yet. It supports the next question: how to represent the exceptional-regime outcome distribution so that productive-survivor upside and real collapse/downside can coexist without compressing both into one central conditional mean.
