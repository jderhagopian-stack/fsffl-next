## Matched-control design
Each frozen high-leverage source coordinate was compared with non-role-loss controls from the same position, source season, and horizon. Five nearest controls were selected using standardized age, experience, and source fantasy points. This is a matching device, not a production threshold.

## Core result
The earlier availability finding does NOT explain the high-leverage collapse sample once controls are matched. High-leverage cases and matched controls have almost identical total non-active share (0.1310 vs 0.1291). Maximum non-active run is only +0.067 weeks and run count +0.052 in cases. Late non-active share and late availability deterioration are actually slightly lower in cases than matched controls.

By contrast, source-time depth-chart role erosion discriminates strongly:
- late depth rank: cases 1.659 vs controls 1.421 (risk-direction difference +0.238)
- last-four-week starter share: cases 0.492 vs controls 0.678 (risk-direction difference +0.186)
- starter-share change: cases -0.0168 vs controls +0.0339 (risk-direction difference +0.0507)

Population depth-rank discrimination is strongly ordered across quartiles:
- Q1 mean rank 1.00 -> 14.8% role-loss
- Q2 mean rank 1.22 -> 21.9%
- Q3 mean rank 1.98 -> 30.3%
- Q4 mean rank 2.76 -> 41.7%

This is continuous/ordered evidence, not a threshold result.

## Position / chronology
Matched late-depth-rank risk difference is strongest and consistent for QB (Y1 +0.252, Y2 +0.265, Y3 +0.383), positive for WR (+0.176/+0.174/+0.118), near-zero for RB after Y1, and adverse/sparse for TE. Therefore any eligibility must be position-aware and must not blanket-apply to all positions.

By source season, late-depth-rank difference is positive in 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2020; near-neutral in 2014 and 2019. This is materially more stable than availability burden, but not perfectly uniform.

## Other families
Age percentile does not discriminate the high-leverage sample from matched controls and is not positively ordered in the population. A simple prior-production-change ratio is U-shaped and unstable, so it is not a clean role-risk signal. Historical point-in-time contract/security and explicit successor-identity evidence are not presently available in a frozen reproducible control source; they cannot be promoted based on collapse-case anecdotes. Roster availability cannot by itself separate temporary injury from persistent physical risk.

## Stage-C recommendation
One family satisfies the Stage-C eligibility requirements strongly enough to carry forward for a formal gate: **continuous, position-aware late depth-chart role/security erosion**, with the evidence strongest for QB and supportive for WR. This family is source-time, historical, reproducible from nflverse weekly depth charts back through the study era, available for current players, discriminates against matched controls, and does not require hindsight.

This recommendation does NOT open Stage C and does NOT authorize fitting. Before any S1/S2 work, management/research must freeze the exact eligible representation and explicitly exclude unsupported blanket RB/TE treatment, contract anecdotes, age penalties, and generic injury burden.
