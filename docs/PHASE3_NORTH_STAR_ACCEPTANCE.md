# FSFFL NEXT — Phase 3 North Star Acceptance Standard

The Phase 3 North Star mockup is a core product target, not a loose style reference. Its visual aesthetic, visual language, page structure, information hierarchy, data formatting, interaction model, and level/type of information shown should guide major Phase 3 surfaces.

## Product-family target

FSFFL NEXT should feel like it belongs in the same product family as the North Star. Visual polish is not a final cleanup pass after functionality. Information architecture, interaction design, data presentation, mobile behavior, and governed backend wiring are designed together.

A surface that passes technical tests but still feels like a derivation of the old beta is not finished.

## Cross-surface visual language

Major Phase 3 surfaces should:

- lead with one strong dominant takeaway rather than an equal-weight card wall;
- use hierarchy, composition, spacing, typography, and graphical emphasis to show what matters first;
- present governed data compactly and graphically where that improves comprehension;
- be intentionally mobile-first rather than a desktop surface compressed to fit;
- keep primary conclusions and primary actions visible without nested disclosure;
- use consistent patterns for strength, weakness, change, risk, opportunity, uncertainty, and action;
- reserve secondary disclosure for provenance, definitions, methodology, and deeper diagnostics;
- feel polished and coherent enough to invite repeated exploration;
- preserve authority boundaries: Presentation can organize and explain governed truth but cannot create model truth.

## Required acceptance check for every major Phase 3 surface

### 1. Visual fidelity

Does the surface feel like the North Star product family?

Check for:
- a clear focal area and dominant takeaway;
- visual hierarchy instead of repeated equal-weight panels;
- deliberate composition, density, typography, and data formatting;
- compact graphical treatments where useful;
- consistent product-wide visual semantics;
- mobile-first layout quality;
- a polished, intentional experience rather than old-beta styling with new data.

### 2. Information fidelity

Does the surface show the most useful governed information for its owner question, rather than merely exposing fields inherited from the prior UI?

Check for:
- the distinct job-to-be-done of the surface;
- authoritative sources used at the correct layer;
- the strongest useful evidence surfaced first;
- missing/uncertain evidence shown honestly;
- no hidden composite score, invented recommendation authority, fake acceptance probability, or presentation-derived model truth;
- reduced duplication with Home and neighboring surfaces;
- information density appropriate to the decision being made.

### 3. Interaction fidelity

Can the user understand what matters and what to do next without digging through several layers?

Check for:
- primary conclusion visible immediately;
- next action obvious where an action is justified;
- minimal nested disclosure for primary content;
- deeper evidence available without overwhelming the first view;
- touch-friendly, mobile-friendly interaction;
- transitions between surfaces that preserve the user's investigative context;
- no dead-end diagnostic panels or decorative controls without a clear user purpose.

## Surface-specific implication

The standard applies to Home, Franchise, League, Market/Opportunities, Trade Center, Owners/Behavioral Intelligence, and later productized surfaces. Each must remain visually coherent with the product family while answering a different question.

Phase 3 work should be judged on all three dimensions together. Technical correctness is necessary but not sufficient.

## Broader roadmap homes to preserve

Phase 3 information architecture must leave intentional future homes for:

- Historical Intelligence, including Historical Trade Grader and point-in-time reconstruction;
- Franchise Timeline and historical causality;
- League History / Museum / Lore;
- Publications and periodical-style reports;
- Analytics and investigation surfaces;
- Scenario / What-If / Alternate History;
- richer Behavioral Intelligence;
- the governed Evidence Warehouse and recalibration infrastructure.

The long-term product journey remains:

`Where am I now? → What changed? → Why? → What could have happened? → What should I do next?`

New Phase 3 navigation or layouts should not consume these future jobs or collapse them into generic dashboards.

## Product-quality checkpoint

After Franchise + League + Market are substantially rebuilt, stop for an explicit product-quality review before treating the Phase 3 direction as validated:

- Does the application materially differ from the old beta?
- Does it visually belong in the North Star family?
- Do Home, Franchise, League, and Market genuinely feel different because they answer different questions?
- Are the primary conclusions immediately legible?
- Has repeated metric/card duplication been reduced?
- Does the mobile experience feel designed, not compressed?
- Is the product enjoyable and useful enough to explore repeatedly?

If the answer is no, correct the product direction before continuing checklist completion.
